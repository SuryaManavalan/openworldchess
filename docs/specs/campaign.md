# Campaign Spec: The Chronicle (the first 15 hours)

**Decided and built** (first version; tuning in §9). A 15-hour arc in which something new opens every 30–60 minutes and the player keeps getting stronger, built almost entirely from systems the game already has. Throughout, players stay free to attack wilds and other players whenever they like: the Chronicle suggests, it never forbids.

## 1. Why players stall today (from a real session)

After an hour, with two cities, a player could neither act nor grow:

| Problem | Cause in the rules | Numbers |
|---|---|---|
| Kings are chained to their cities | A building with no king within 10 squares stops producing after 60 s, then loses 1 HP every 30 s | Leaving to hunt makes the city "die" within a minute |
| Waiting for a king takes "forever" | Kings only come from a palace; a palace needs ore **and** rock in its work area; each extra king makes crowning slower | Ore is in reach at ~8% of starting sites; a palace makes a king every ~40–60 min once it exists |
| Beating a horde feels empty | The aftermath is: camp scattered, land cleared | No reward you can hold, see or use |

Nothing new to *do* opens up after the first hour: all five buildings are available from minute one, so there is nothing left to discover.

## 2. Principles

1. **One track, five small systems, one quest vocabulary.** The Chronicle is a single visible path of chapters, and quests (§5) lead the player through it. Everything it unlocks is either an existing thing revealed at the right moment, or one of five small systems (§4), each explainable in one sentence.
2. **Every chapter gives both a *new verb or place* and *more power*.** Novelty alone is a tutorial; power alone is a grind.
3. **Goals use actions players already take:** build, march, hunt, trade, settle, fight. The Chronicle points at things (a bounty marker, a palace site, a rare biome) rather than inventing chores.
4. **Kings are the unit of power.** More kings means more armies, more cities, more freedom. The campaign's main reward is a king at a steady pace: about one per chapter pair early on, and every chapter later.
5. **Never block free play.** Attack anyone, anytime. Chapter goals can be done in any order within an act, and skipping ahead is allowed (§8).

## 3. The arc at a glance

Five acts, 15 chapters. Times are for a player of middling pace; fast players go quicker and are never held back.

**The story (as rewritten on 2026-09-28).** Long ago the **First Empire** covered the whole Board, until the wilds swallowed it and its crowns were scattered. The player carries one of those crowns. The campaign is the tale of rebuilding: guard the Emperor (Act I), win back crowns and make new ones (Act II), push into the frontier the First Empire lost (Act III), meet the other crown-bearers (Act IV), and finally face the Dragon that ended the First Empire and build what will outlast you (Act V). Each chapter opens with a short **story card** (`story` in `CHAPTERS`), and each step's line is written in the Chronicle's voice.

**Teaching through the story.** Every step names the rules it teaches (`teach`, §5.7), so the player learns each mechanic at the moment they first need it: the Emperor, reach and resources with the first house; production, room and bubbles with the first pawns; kings, leading a king away, and moving pieces when chapter 1 sends the king (never the Emperor) to scout the camp that chapter 2 then hunts.

## 4. The five systems (everything new)

### 4.1 The Chronicle (titles, coronations, bounties)

- **Chapters:** a panel (the book icon) shows the current chapter's 1–3 goals, the unlock waiting at the end, and a marker on the map for any goal with a place (the herd to hunt, the palace site, the rare land).
- **Titles** are the power ladder. Each raises the player's **king cap** and adds **+6 population per king**:

  | Title | King cap |
  |---|---|
  | Chieftain | 2 |
  | Warden | 3 |
  | Lord | 3 |
  | Baron | 4 |
  | Count | 5 |
  | Duke | 6 |
  | Prince | 8 |
  | King | 12 |
  | High King | 20 (today's hard cap) |

  The title shows beside your name and on your king banners.
- **Coronation:** at chapters 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 13 and 14, a new king appears beside your Emperor, with a short ceremony (the town bell rings and a crown glints). The king counts toward the cap, is not part of the starting kit, and can be lost in battle as usual.
- **Renown** is a single number earned by victories (wild camps by rarity, battles against empires, sieges) and shown on your profile. It is **not a currency**: it only feeds chapter goals ("earn 300 Renown in the wilds") and bragging rights.
- **Bounties:** each chapter that asks for a hunt marks a suitable camp nearby, sized to the player's army.

### 4.2 Spoils of the wild (what beating a camp gives you)

When a camp's king falls, the camp scatters as now, and leaves one or more of:

- **A Hoard,** always: a treasure-cache resource node where the camp stood.
  - Its kind depends on the faction: gold coins for kobolds and dragons (counts as ore), a timber pile for wolves, a grain store for herds, cut stone for goblins and trolls.
  - Its capacity is 3–10× a normal node's, scaling with camp size and rarity, and it runs out.
  - The land a camp sat on becomes a *great* city site. That's the reason to clear camps near home, and it's how players without ore get their first palace.
- **Captives,** from raider camps (bandits, orcs, goblins, gnolls, sahuagin): 1–4 pawns, and sometimes a knight, in *your* color. They were people the raiders took, which is why they join. This fits "creatures never join you": captives were never creatures.
- **A Relic,** from rare and legendary camps: a trophy set in your capital's plaza (a dragon skull, a crystal crown, a sphinx head). Each relic gives its town a small production bonus (+10%), shows on the map, and is named on your profile. Relics are unique per faction per player, so collecting them is a goal of its own.
- **Renown** by the camp's size and rarity.

Losing to a camp costs what it costs today (your king and the pieces with it) and gives the camp nothing.

### 4.3 Holding the realm (fixing the chain between kings and cities)

One rule replaces "a king must always be there":

- **Settlements hold themselves by tier.** Once no king is in reach, a settlement keeps producing for a while, then pauses production. It never decays while it has any of your pieces inside.

  | Tier | Keeps producing without a king for |
  |---|---|
  | Hamlet | 10 min |
  | Village | 1 h |
  | Town | 4 h |
  | City | 12 h |
  | Your capital | Forever |

  Decay (losing HP) happens only when a settlement has neither a king nor any of your pieces.
- **Regents** (from chapter 5): a queen in a settlement holds it like a king. That gives queens a peaceful job, and leaving one at home frees your king to adventure.
- **Walls muster** (from chapter 10): a walled town's defense picks its set from 14 squares, not 10, so besieged cities fight with their whole garrison.
- **Capital** (from chapter 11): one town, chosen once, holds itself forever and crowns kings 25% faster.

### 4.4 Roads and trade (existing traffic and merchants given a purpose)

- **Roads speed marches** (from chapter 7): a troop whose lead point is on a street (traffic of 60 or more) advances 1.5× per turn. The roads players wear by walking become highways. **Paved roads** that knights lay ([movement.md](movement.md) §9) give everyone the same speed from the start; this unlock extends it to busy streets.
- **Trade** (from chapter 6): towns linked by a working merchant road produce 15% faster (at most +30% per town). The existing caravans become an economic reason to have two towns close together.

### 4.5 The Wonder (the one new building)

- **One per empire,** in your capital. It costs 400 wood, 400 stone and 150 ore, so most players will need hoards to afford it. (An earlier draft required a relic; relics mostly come from high-rated lands, so that could make the capstone unreachable. Relics stay a bonus.)
- **Built over 30 minutes** of anchored construction.
- **Each civilization has its own:** a great temple for Dravidian, a colosseum for Roman, a pagoda tower for Chinese, a pyramid for Egyptian, and a classic citadel for the default style. Cosmetic civilizations (cosmetics.md) extend here naturally.
- **Effects:**
  - It's visible on everyone's far map.
  - Its town produces 25% faster.
  - The player earns the *High King* title and a permanent Wonder emblem on their banners.

## 5. Quests: how players move through the Chronicle

The Chronicle is *what* happens over 15 hours; quests are *how* a player is led through it, one clear next step at a time. Quests are the only instructions the game gives, so they replace the separate onboarding guide (its four steps become Chapter 1's quest).

### 5.1 Two kinds, never more than four at once

- **The main quest:** always exactly one, the current chapter's goals as a short story.
  - Each chapter is 2–3 steps, shown one at a time.
  - Each step has a line from the Chronicle, the scribe-narrator whose book is the quest icon, and a concrete target: *"The deer of Mossglade grow bold. Hunt the Stag Herd east of your village."*
  - Finishing the last step completes the chapter: the unlock ceremony plays and the next chapter's first step appears.
- **Side quests,** up to **three** at once, offers included (as of 2026-09-28; one offer waits at a time): short, optional errands **generated from the world around you** (§5.3).
  - They pay Renown and small, useful rewards.
  - They fill the gaps between chapters and send you toward things you'd enjoy anyway.
  - Each arrives as an **offer** you accept or decline (§5.5). Nothing is lost by declining: a declined or dropped quest is offered again about 20 minutes of play later, if it still makes sense.

There is no quest log to manage, no quest chains to track and no fetch-and-carry. If a quest can't be expressed with the game's existing verbs (§5.2), it doesn't exist.

### 5.2 One small vocabulary of goals

Every quest step is one of these, all checked on the server from events it already produces:

| Verb | Example step | Checked from |
|---|---|---|
| **Build** (type, count) | "Raise a stable" | A building finished |
| **Grow** (a settlement to a tier) | "Make Mossglade a village" | A settlement's tier |
| **Raise** (piece kind, count) | "Raise 2 knights" | A piece produced |
| **March** (to a place, or a distance) | "Scout the ford to the north" | Your king reaching a square |
| **Hunt** (camp by temper, size or rarity) | "Clear the Goblin Camp at the quarry" | A camp scattered by you |
| **Free** (captives) | "Free the orcs' prisoners" | Captives released |
| **Settle** (a new settlement: distance, rating or biome) | "Found a town on the ore hills" | A new settlement's first building |
| **Link** (two towns by trade) | "Let a caravan reach Ashford" | A merchant arriving |
| **Discover** (a biome or faction) | "Find the Blossom Vale" | A square of that biome in view |
| **Win** (a battle: field, siege, or against an empire) | "Defeat a rival's troop" | Battle results |
| **Promote** (a pawn in battle) | "Carry a pawn to the far rank" | A promotion |
| **Crown** (a king, via a palace or coronation) | "Crown a king in your palace" | A king produced |

Steps carry a **target and a map marker** whenever there's a place: a camp, a site, a biome edge. A **Show me** button flies the camera there, as the onboarding guide does now.

### 5.3 Side quests from the world around you

From chapter 2, 10 minutes into play and then every 12 minutes, while you hold fewer than three quests (offers included) and none is waiting for an answer, the server offers one side quest (as of 2026-09-28; it was every 30 minutes).

**Pacing (as of 2026-09-28).** Each kind opens at a chapter, and some need what you've built. Among the open kinds, the ones that suit the chapter's current step come first, so side quests follow the story: a hunting step brings bounties, rescues and feats; building and growing bring growth quests and the pilgrimage; marching and settling bring scouting, shrines and the pilgrimage; the Rivals and Siegecraft chapters bring skirmishes, openings and feats. You never hold two of the same kind.

| Kind | Opens at chapter | Also needs |
|---|---|---|
| Bounty | 2, First Hunt | a camp you can beat nearby |
| Grow | 3, Village | a settlement one building short of its next tier |
| Shrine | 3 | open land nearby |
| Feat | 4 | |
| Rescue, Opening | 5 | a rescue needs a raider camp you can beat |
| Pilgrimage | 5 | temples open, and a village or bigger |
| Scout | 7, Roads Beyond | an unseen rare land |
| Skirmish | 10, Rivals | a rival empire nearby | A declined quest that's due comes back first; otherwise it writes a new one from the player's surroundings. It picks the first template that fits, rotating so the same kind never comes twice in a row:

| Template | When it fits | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| **Bounty** | A camp within 150 squares that your army can beat (size ≤ your largest troop + 2) | Renown ×1.5 and a bigger hoard |
| **Rescue** | A raider camp with captives nearby | Its captives, plus one extra pawn |
| **Scout** | An unseen rare biome within 400 squares, or land you haven't seen that rates 200+ higher than yours | Renown, and the land marked on your map |
| **Caravan** | Two of your towns within trade range that aren't linked | Trade starts at +20% for an hour |
| **Grow** | A settlement one building short of the next tier | One free piece from its best building |
| **Skirmish** | A rival empire (bot or person) within 200 squares, not shielded, of similar strength | Renown ×2 for the win |
| **Defend** | A horde whose roaming passes near your town | Renown, and the camp's full hoard |
| **Shrine** | Open land 20–45 squares from a king | 60 Renown and a bishop |
| **Opening** | Always | 70 Renown |
| **Feat** | Always | 60 Renown and a knight |
| **Pilgrimage** (a chain of 3) | Temples are open, and a grove of 10+ trees lies 30–45 squares from your capital (or biggest town), away from other empires | 150 Renown and a bishop |

Three chess quests (as built on 2026-09-28):
- **Shrine:** an old shrine of the game stands in the wild (only its quest-holder sees it). Walk any piece onto it and it poses a **riddle**: a mate in 1 (players under 1100) or a mate in 2, with exactly one first move. Answer it on a small board. The server checks every move by brute force: a right move gets the defense's toughest reply, and a wrong one resets the riddle (try again as often as you like).
  - The riddles come from `tools/puzzles/gen.mjs`: Stockfish plays weak games to find candidates, one per game, and each is proved by chess.js brute force (exactly one first move forces mate in n, none mates faster). They're stored in `packages/shared/src/puzzles.json`.
- **Opening:** "The monks of the east honor only the Queen's Gambit": win a battle as White having played the opening's moves among your first few (the Italian, the Queen's Gambit, the London, the King's Gambit, the English, the Ruy Lopez, and the Bongcloud).
- **Feat:** win a battle with a handicap: without losing your queen, in 20 moves or fewer, after promoting a pawn, or losing no more than two pieces.
- Openings and feats are judged from the battle's own moves when it ends (`Chronicle.battleWon`); wins against empires and camps both count.

The **pilgrimage** (as built on 2026-09-28) teaches what pieces do outside battle, in three stages:
1. **Clear the grove** with elephants: 80% of its trees down (movement.md §9). Progress shows as trees cleared.
2. **Raise an altar** in the clearing, with a bishop beside it (economy.md §8).
3. **Pave a road** with knights from the altar to one of your towns (movement.md §9). It's done when paved squares link the altar to within 10 squares of a town.

Rewards stay small and **never outgrow the main quest**. Side quests give variety and a nudge, not a second progression track. They're also how the world keeps offering something after the campaign ends (§8, the Epilogue).

### 5.4 Adapting to the player

- **Sized to your army:** hunts target camps you can beat (as above), and the main quest's hunts are chosen among camps near your size.
- **Placed near you:** targets within a few minutes' march of a king whenever possible, so a quest is a short trip, not a pilgrimage.
- **Never stuck:**
  - If a step's target disappears (someone else clears the camp, a town falls), it's re-targeted at once.
  - If a step hasn't moved for 45 minutes of play, the Chronicle offers an easier alternative that counts the same: "Or: clear any two camps".
- **Doing things early counts:** finishing a later step's goal early banks it, and it completes the moment its chapter arrives.

### 5.5 Presentation

- **Tracker:** a compact card, top left (where the guide sits now), with the main step, its progress ("2/3 knights") and Show me.
  - It **folds down** to a one-line pill (the chevron), and opens again with a tap; the choice is remembered. On phones it takes one line instead of a card.
  - **Side quests live in the Chronicle** (as of 2026-09-28), at the top of the book: offers with *Where?*, **Decline** (tap twice; it says the quest will come back) and **Accept**, then your quests with *Where?*, *Drop* and **Follow**. The Chronicle button shows a badge while a quest is offered, and a short alert says so once.
  - **Following** a side quest puts it in the banner (its progress, Show, a how-to tip for the pilgrimage, *Drop*), with a chip back to the chapter. It's remembered across reloads.
  - Every control is at least 36–44px tall, with no small close buttons beside a list (a thumb reaching to close one used to decline a quest by accident).
- **Help for every quest** (as of 2026-09-28): **How?** on the banner (and on each side quest in the Chronicle) opens a card with numbered steps, **what to look for** drawn in this land's own art and named locally (in a taiga the crops are berry bushes), and buttons that do the fiddly part: *Show me the crops* (flies there and rings the nearest), *Place a house for me* (puts the outline on a good spot; you confirm), *Select my army*, *Show me the camp*. Code: `apps/client/src/ui/QuestHelp.tsx`.
- **Sheets:** every bottom sheet has a full-width handle: tap it, or swipe it down (the sheet follows your finger), to close.
- **Map:** quest targets get a gold pin that joins the existing marker system (flags, settlement pins, edge arrows), so off-screen targets show as edge arrows with distances.
- **Moments:**
  - A finished step gives a soft chime and a check mark.
  - A finished chapter gets the **unlock ceremony**: a full-width card with the new building or ability in art, the bell, and "New: …".
  - A coronation shows the crown descending on the new king in the world.
- **Phone:** the tracker collapses to one line above the troop bar; tapping it expands the steps.

### 5.7 Lessons: how the rules are taught (as built 2026-09-28)

Every rule a player must understand is a **lesson** in `packages/shared/src/lessons.ts`, in three layers:
- **text:** the one thing to know, said plainly ("Send your king, never your Emperor").
- **fine print:** the complete rule, with every number, exception and interaction, so a player who reads it all has no doubts. Nothing about the game is a secret.
- **tactics:** how to play it well, including the strategy that emerges from the rules (when to attack, what to bring, where to build).

Numbers are read from the rules' own constants (reach, hold and decay times, room per building, king caps, countdowns, clocks, production speed), so a changed constant changes the lesson with it. There are 36 lessons, covering the Emperor, kings, reach, leading a king away, resources, building (with every building's cost, needs and speed), production, room, bubbles, the building cap, moving pieces, battles, the army you bring, what battles cost, rest, the wilds, raids and troops without a king, masterless pieces and ruins, settlements, holding a town, making kings, ore, trade, clearing, paving, altars, rivals, the land's rating, your rating, promotion, sieges and walls, hoards and captives, relics, the capital, the Wonder, and Renown and titles.
- **The rulebook** (Help, and *Read the full rulebook* in the Chronicle) lists every lesson, taught yet or not.
- **A test** (`campaign.test.ts`) checks that every lesson is taught by some quest step or side quest (`SIDE_TEACH`), that every lesson a quest names exists, and that each has its plain line and fine print.
- **Steps teach lessons:** a step lists the lessons it teaches (`teach: ['emperor', 'reach']`). The quest banner shows them as a *Learn:* chip; the step's help card (**How?**) explains them under *Why it works this way*; and the Chronicle's book keeps every lesson met so far under *What you've learned*.
- **Changing or adding a rule** is a data change: edit the lesson (or add one) and name it in the `teach` of the step where a player first meets it. Side quests have their own lesson lists in the help card.
- **New step kinds** for teaching by doing: `scout` (a king, not the Emperor, looks upon the marked camp), `clear` (trees felled by your elephants), `pave` (squares paved by your knights).

**Winning feels like winning.** A finished step or side quest plays a jackpot: a gold flash, fireworks of coins, gems and confetti, a slammed "QUEST COMPLETE" with a shine, the Renown counting up, and a slot-machine run of notes into a chord of bells (a chapter adds a gong and the choir, then the ceremony). Code: `apps/client/src/ui/Celebrate.tsx`, `Audio.jackpot`.

### 5.6 Data and rules (server)

- **Quests are data,** not code. Each chapter is a list of steps (`verb`, `params`, `line`, `reward`); side quests are templates plus a picker. They live in `packages/shared/src/chronicle.ts`, so the client can render them, and the server evaluates them from events it already logs (`logEvent`, battle summaries, building completions).
- **Per player,** the state is: current chapter and step, banked goals, up to three side quests, Renown, title. It's saved with the player record.
- **Bots** take quests too (one more utility in their brain), so they move through the Chronicle like people do, and the world's other empires grow at a believable pace.
- **Rewards go through the same code as the systems they belong to:** hoards through Spoils, kings through Coronation, pieces through normal production (they respect population caps).

## 7. The first-session fixes (they don't wait for the Chronicle)

- **Starting beside grown camps** (as of 2026-09-28): new players aren't placed within 35 squares of a camp of more than 8 pieces (camps grow with the empires around them, so a spot beside an old empire can be ringed by full sets). And every new empire gets **a young band of its own** nearby: a small camp under 80% of its strength that never grows, so the first hunt always has something beatable.

These address the stall directly, and should ship first:

1. **Holding the realm** (§4.3): tier grace, and no decay while your pieces are home.
2. **Spoils** (§4.2), starting with Hoards and Renown.
3. **A faster first palace king:** the first king from each player's first palace takes 10 minutes. Later kings take the normal time, including the per-king slowdown.
4. **Newcomers' grace (added after the balance simulation):** through chapter 3, a king beaten by a wild camp isn't killed. It retreats wounded, with 10 minutes of recovery, and its army regroups. In the simulation, players who lost their spare king to a camp in chapter 2 were left unable to hunt at all.
   - **Strength is the legal set.** A side fields at most 8 pawns, 2 knights, 2 bishops, 2 rooks and a queen (`setWorth` in shared). Hunt targets, side quests and bots' attack choices all compare this, not a raw piece count. In the simulation, chapter-2 realms of 40 pawns and knights were being sent against camps with queens and rooks, and lost every time.
   - **Young bands for hunts.** If a plain hunt has no camp in reach that the player could beat, the Chronicle raises a young band for them (`Wilds.quarry`). It stands 16–30 squares from their strongest king, has under 80% of their strength, and never grows. Each player gets one at a time.
   - **A full population never blocks the chapter's piece.** A building can raise the piece the current step asks for past the population cap, up to the number still needed.
   - **Result (8 bots, 3 game hours):** every bot finished chapters 1–3, and 6 of 8 finished chapter 4. The median time to reach each chapter's end was 4.5, 24, 27 and 56 game-minutes.
5. **Coronations for existing players:** a one-time grant, so an active player of one hour or more gets a third king at once.

## 8. Existing players, bots and freedom

- **Existing players** are placed at the chapter their holdings match (buildings, kings, settlements), and receive every unlock before it. Nobody loses a building they already have.
- **Bots** follow the Chronicle too (it gives them goals, which makes them read as humans): their brain gains "pursue the current chapter" as one more utility.
- **Freedom:** chapter goals can be done early and in any order within an act. Completing a later chapter's goal early banks it. Attacking anyone, anytime, stays as it is. Buildings unlocked by chapters (stable, temple, barracks, palace) also unlock the first time you capture one.

## 9. Measuring it

- **Per player:** the server records the time each chapter and each quest step completes (active play only), and which side quests are declined. Often-declined templates get cut or reworked. `/metrics` gains the median and 90th-percentile time per chapter, so slow chapters show up.
- **Bot simulation:** a benchmark runs 20 bots through the Chronicle at high speed. Any chapter whose median time falls outside its window in §3 gets retuned: goal sizes, hoard sizes, coronation spacing.
- **The feel test:** at no point should more than an hour pass with nothing new. The simulation flags any gap between unlocks longer than 60 minutes of play.

## 10. Build order

| Phase | What | Why first |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Holding the realm (§4.3 tiers only); Spoils (Hoards, Renown); faster first palace king | Fixes the stall reported in play |
| 2 | Quest engine (§5: main quest, tracker, markers, Show me); Chronicle chapters 1–6; titles, coronations; gated buildings for new players; the onboarding guide becomes Chapter 1 | The spine of the campaign and the first three hours |
| 3 | Side quests (§5.3); Captives, Relics; Regents; Trade; Roads speed; Cartography; chapters 7–10 | The middle hours |
| 4 | Walls muster, Capital, Muster; chapters 11–14 | Dominion |
| 5 | The Wonder (art for 5 styles); chapter 15; the Epilogue (side quests continue indefinitely, with rotating weekly chapters) | Capstone |

## 11. What this deliberately doesn't add

No second currency (Renown only counts), no skill trees, no crafting, no new piece kinds, no timers to rush, no pay-to-win: the shop stays cosmetic. The only new building is the Wonder. Everything else deepens something players already see: camps, settlements, roads, merchants, walls, palaces and queens.

- **Bots and performance:**
  - Bot quests reuse existing orders, rate-limited as before (bots get one pathfinding order every 2 seconds).
  - Bots never subscribe to far areas to chase a quest target.
  - Quest target searches are cached per player: 10 minutes for map searches, 30 seconds for camps and rivals.
  - The Chronicle's tick runs every 5 seconds and shows in `/metrics` as `chronicle`.
- **Balance simulation:** a fresh world with 8 bots at 6× speed (economy, turns, countdowns and bot thinking all scaled). Chapter times come from `chron.doneAt` in game minutes. Battles still run on real chess clocks, so battle-heavy chapters read slow in the simulation.
