# Audio Spec: The Cherry on Top

**Decided:** sound should be the cherry on top. It should add to the living, breathing world, make every interaction satisfying, and be immersive enough that players feel they've entered the world. The reference is the pull of a casino: rewards that land physically.

Visual twin: [visuals.md](visuals.md).

## 1. Pillars

1. **Everything is music.** The world runs at **100 BPM** (one world turn = one beat; [visuals.md](visuals.md) §1). Footsteps, work sounds and rewards land on the beat, and everything is **in the key of the current music**. Nothing in the world sounds out of tune with anything else.
2. **Armies are rhythm sections.** Each piece type is a percussion voice (§3), so a troop's composition becomes a groove. You can *hear* an army: pawns ticking like snares, an elephant's kick, knights clopping like woodblocks.
3. **Rewards cascade.** Bigger wins produce longer, brighter, rising runs of notes, with visuals exactly in sync.
4. **Sound comes from the world.** Ambience comes from what's really on screen and near the camera. Zoom in and you hear the forge; zoom out and you hear the wind across a continent.
5. **It never tires you out.** Every sound has several variants and small random pitch shifts, repeats are capped, loudness is normalized, and there are full volume controls.

## 2. The musical world

- **Palette:** modal and medieval, with a hint of the mystical.
  - Instruments: lute, hurdy-gurdy drone, frame drums, low horns, choir pads, bells, hammered dulcimer.
  - Home mode: **D Dorian**, which modulates as the game state changes.
- **Adaptive music with layered parts:** each part is a separate recording (a stem), brought in or out by what's happening.

| State | Layers |
|---|---|
| Exploring, calm | drone + soft dulcimer and lute melodies + ambience |
| Your troops moving | adds a frame-drum pulse, which grows with the number of troops moving |
| Enemies nearby (a hostile king within 20 squares of your pieces) | a low horn drone and tension percussion |
| Attack countdown (60s / 15s) | a heartbeat drum that **speeds up** toward the start, and a rising drone |
| Battle | sparse and focused: a pulse at clock tempo, chess clacks woven into the rhythm, the melody thinning as clocks run low |
| Victory / defeat | a fanfare in the major mode / a solemn horn in the minor mode |

- **Endless variety:** composed stems are layered with a **generative ornament layer**. Phrases are generated by rules in the current key and over the current chord, so long sessions never loop audibly. That's the hypnotic, lean-back quality.

## 3. Sounds of the pieces (on the beat)

| Piece | Movement sound | Rhythm role |
|---|---|---|
| Pawn | a short boot step; an about-face stomp | snare / tick |
| Knight | a hoof clop; a whoosh on the hop; a thud on landing | woodblock |
| Bishop | a robe swish and a tiny bell | high bell |
| Elephant | a heavy footfall; an occasional low trumpet | kick drum |
| Queen | a silky glide with a shimmer | shaker / chime |
| King / Emperor | a heavy cape step; the Emperor adds a low horn when it sets out | low tom |

- **Mixing large armies:** the steps of the same piece type in one troop merge into one sound, with more layers as numbers grow, up to 4 voices. A large army sounds big without turning into noise.
- **Idle life** ([visuals.md](visuals.md) §2): sickles swishing in time, the forge anvil landing on the downbeat, pawn drills stamping on the beat, bells at the temple. Together they make the settlement's groove, and every settlement sounds slightly different, depending on its buildings.

## 4. Interaction sounds (the satisfying part)

| Action | Sound |
|---|---|
| Select one piece | a soft plucked note in the current key |
| **Lasso-select a group** | each piece plucks the next note up the scale as it's selected, an **ascending run**. Twelve pieces make a satisfying cascade. |
| Path preview | a quiet rising shimmer that follows your finger |
| Commit a move | a warm "thunk" plus the current chord |
| Declare an attack | a horn blast and a low drum hit |
| Place a building | three hammer taps on the beat |
| Construction finished | a bell and a short chime flourish |
| Production done | a bell. Completions **within 10 seconds of each other climb in pitch** (a ladder that resets after 10s quiet) |
| **Conversion wave** | each banner flip is a chime in an **ascending cascade, like a payout**, with a choir swell. It's the biggest reward in the game and should feel that way. |
| Rating up | a short fanfare flourish |
| Error or blocked | a soft, low muted pluck; never harsh |

### Battle (chess)

- **Moves:** crisp wooden clacks, several variations. They're close to chess.com's feel because players know it, but recorded fresh.
- Capture: a heavier clack plus a metallic ring; bystanders in the crowd murmur.
- **Castling:** a double clack.
- Check: a short string sting.
- Promotion: a rising chime.
- Clock under 10 seconds: a tick, integrated into the music.
- **Checkmate:** a gong, a choir swell, and the victory or defeat cue.

## 5. Ambience

- **Biome beds:**
  - grassland: wind, birds;
  - forest: leaves, a woodpecker, a creek;
  - river: flowing water;
  - mountains: a hollow wind with echo;
  - settlement: murmur, hammering, wheels, footsteps;
  - night: crickets, an owl, a distant bell.

  They crossfade with the time of day and weather: rain on roofs, thunder far off.
- **Placement in space:** each sound pans across the stereo field according to its position on screen. Distance and zoom soften it (a low-pass filter). Each biome has its own reverb: damp in forests, echoing in mountains, and a warm, enclosed room inside the battle dome.
- **Reacting to the world:** birds scattering, a deer bolting, splashes at fords. Whatever reacts on screen ([visuals.md](visuals.md) §4) makes a sound.

## 6. Mixing and comfort

- **Categories:** master, music, ambience, world effects, UI, battle, each with its own volume.
- **Focus mode** plays battle sounds only.
- **Ducking:** UI sounds briefly lower the ambience; a battle lowers the world; an alert cuts through everything.
- Loudness is normalized to −16 LUFS, the level used on mobile, and nothing sudden is ever loud. **Variety:** 4–8 recordings of each sound, pitch shifted ±3%, and the same recording is never used twice in a row.
- **Haptics on mobile** are paired with key sounds: move commits, captures, check, the conversion wave, and attack alerts ([ux.md](ux.md) §4).

## 7. Casino craft, used carefully

We **borrow** casino-level sensory craft:
- runs of notes that climb in pitch;
- payout cascades;
- perfect sync between audio and visuals;
- the sense that every touch rewards you.

We **don't borrow** the manipulative mechanics:
- no near-miss teasers;
- no dressing up a loss as a win;
- no sounds that guilt you for leaving;
- no rewards just for time spent.

**Hurry bubbles** ([economy.md](economy.md) §7) use this craft: a climbing combo, gold surprises, instant feedback on every touch. They stay on the right side of the line:
- they appear only while you're playing, and at most 3 wait per building, so there's nothing to come back for;
- a pop always pays the same, and gold is shown before you tap it;
- no notifications about them.

The world should be absorbing because it's alive and satisfying, not because it exploits compulsion. That keeps player trust, avoids trouble with app store and regulatory rules on manipulative design, and the world doesn't need it.

## 8. Technology

- **Tone.js** (MIT) on the Web Audio API:
  - **Transport** is locked to the server's world-turn clock, so the beat matches animations and the server ([networking.md](networking.md) §1 `hello` provides turn timing).
  - **Sampler and Player** handle recorded sounds; synths handle UI plucks at first; built-in effects handle reverb and filtering.
  - Pitch quantization comes from a small key and chord service driven by the music state.
- **Timing:** world sounds are scheduled to the beat (up to 100ms ahead). **UI sounds play immediately.** A touch must never wait for the beat. Only its tail (a chord or cascade) aligns to the beat.
- **Formats:** AAC/m4a (needed for iOS) with Opus/WebM for other browsers. Sounds are packed into audio sprites.
  - First load: under 2 MB of audio (UI + core Foley).
  - Music stems and biome beds stream in lazily.
- **Voice budget:** 32 voices at once, with per-category limits, lowest priority dropped first.
- **Mobile:**
  - The audio context unlocks on the first touch.
  - Audio suspends in the background.
  - Under Capacitor, the iOS audio session is set so the silent switch behaves as players expect ([TECH.md](../TECH.md) T18).

## 9. Production

- **Day 0 prototype:** synthesized UI sounds (Tone synths) plus CC0 / royalty-free libraries (Kenney, the Sonniss GDC bundles) for Foley and ambience. That's enough to build and tune the system.
- **Before the alpha:** commission a composer (the stems and generative rules) and a sound designer (Foley for the pieces, rewards, chess clacks, the settlement grooves).
- **Everything is data-driven:** a sound-event table maps each game event to sound assets and rules (variants, pitch, beat quantization, ducking). Swapping in final audio means editing data, not code.
